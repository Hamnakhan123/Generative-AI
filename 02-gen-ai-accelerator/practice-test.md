## Part B · Scenario questions (3)

### 🧩 Scenario 1 · Choosing tools for a department newsletter

Your department wants a monthly newsletter: a 600-word lead article with two cited statistics, a poster-style cover image, and a short audio version for the department's social page. The budget is zero and student names may appear in the content. Recommend tools by category, explain your choice with C-FIPS, and describe the safeguards.

<details><summary>✅ Model solution</summary>

**Tools by category (free tiers):**

1. **Article:** a chat assistant (ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot or Claude) for the draft, and a **research tool** (Perplexity or NotebookLM) to find the two statistics with sources.
2. **Cover image:** an image/design tool with free credits (Canva Magic Studio or Adobe Firefly), which also handle text layout.
3. **Audio:** a text-to-speech tool's free tier (e.g. ElevenLabs), with the voice clearly labelled as AI-generated.

**C-FIPS reasoning:** *Capability* — each tool is used for its strength; *Free tier* — all have free plans that cover a monthly issue; *Integration* — Canva and Google/Microsoft assistants fit existing workflows; *Privacy* — turn off training on chats and avoid pasting identifiable details; *Sources* — statistics come only from a tool that links real sources, then each link is opened and checked.

**Safeguards:**

- Replace student names with placeholders while drafting; add real names (with consent) only in the final document.
- Verify both statistics with V-3 (open the source, lateral reading, cross-check).
- Human review of the full newsletter before publishing; add a one-line AI-use disclosure.
- Check each tool's terms for commercial or public use of free-tier outputs.

</details>


### 🧩 Scenario 2 · The student who "studied" with AI

A student says: "I used AI for every assignment this term and got good marks, but I failed the mid-term." Diagnose what likely went wrong and design a 2-week AI-supported study routine that fixes it, staying within academic-integrity rules.

<details><summary>✅ Model solution</summary>

**Diagnosis:** the student used AI in **answer mode** — AI did the thinking, so there was no retrieval practice and no feedback on their own understanding. Possibly also an integrity problem if work was submitted as their own.

**2-week routine:**

1. **Days 1–2:** paste lecture notes into an assistant and generate 15 flashcards per topic; check every answer against the notes.
2. **Daily (20 min):** active recall — "Quiz me one question at a time using ONLY these notes"; track scores.
3. **Leitner schedule:** right answers move up a box (review after 1, 3, 7, 14 days); wrong answers go back to box 1.
4. **Every other day:** Feynman check — explain a concept in 5 sentences and ask AI to find gaps; rewrite.
5. **Practice problems:** Socratic tutor prompt — hints only, never full solutions; interleave topics.
6. **Day 12:** timed mock test with no AI; mark it, then use AI only to explain mistakes.
7. **Integrity:** follow the course AI policy, disclose permitted help, never submit AI-written work as their own.

</details>


### 🧩 Scenario 3 · An AI admissions assistant

A university plans an AI chatbot to answer applicants' questions and pre-screen applications for a scholarship. Identify the main risks, score the top two with a risk matrix, and propose controls using responsible-AI principles and human-in-the-loop design.

<details><summary>✅ Model solution</summary>

**Main risks:** hallucinated fees or deadlines; biased pre-screening; personal-data exposure; lack of disclosure that applicants are talking to AI; over-reliance by staff; outage at peak times.

**Risk matrix (likelihood × impact, 1–5):**

| Risk | L | I | Score | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hallucinated fee/deadline | 4 | 5 | 20 | HIGH |
| Biased scholarship pre-screening | 3 | 5 | 15 | HIGH |

**Controls:**

- **Reliability:** answer fees and deadlines only from approved documents (retrieval), show the source link, and say "confirm on the official page".
- **Fairness:** run a four-fifths-rule audit on pre-screening results by group before launch and every term; remove proxies for protected characteristics.
- **Human oversight:** the AI only *ranks* applications; a scholarship committee makes every decision (**human-in-the-loop**); applicants can request human review.
- **Transparency:** tell users they are chatting with AI and how their data is used.
- **Privacy:** collect the minimum data, use an enterprise plan with no training on inputs, set retention limits.
- **Accountability:** name an owner, log decisions, and review incidents monthly. Pre-screening for scholarships would count as high-risk under frameworks like the EU AI Act, so document testing and oversight.

</details>
