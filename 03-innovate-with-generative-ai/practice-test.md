## Part B · Scenario questions (3)

### 🧩 Scenario 1 · A launch content pack for a campus app

Your society is launching "CanteenQ", an app to pre-order canteen food. In one week you must produce: a 600-word blog post, an Instagram carousel caption, an event poster (4:5), and a 30-second promo reel with captions. Budget is zero. Describe the tools by modality, the prompts you would use, the review checks for each deliverable, and how you would keep the brand consistent.

<details><summary>✅ Model solution</summary>

**Tools by modality (free tiers):** chat assistant for the blog and captions; an image tool (Firefly/Ideogram/Canva) for poster artwork; a design tool (Canva/PowerPoint) for poster text; TTS + CapCut for the reel.

**Prompts:**

- *Blog (RTACFC):* "You are a friendly tech writer. Write a 600-word blog titled 'Skip the canteen queue with CanteenQ' for first-year students. Context: """[features, launch date, price]""". Format: H1, 80-word intro, four H2 sections, CTA. Rules: no invented statistics, sentences under 20 words." Drafted section by section with a word budget.
- *Caption:* "Three captions under 60 words, one emoji each, end with 'Download: link in bio', two hashtags."
- *Poster art:* "Flat vector illustration of students holding phones with food icons, canteen background, warm lighting, empty space in the top third, palette navy/orange/gold, no text --ar 4:5. Negative prompt: text, watermark."
- *Reel:* 70-word script (hook, problem, solution, proof, CTA), timed at 150 wpm (≈ 28 s), six 4–6 s shots with one visual prompt each.

**Review checks:** blog: facts, prices, dates, readability 60–70, keyword density; caption: platform length, hashtags, CTA; poster: spelling, WCAG contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for details and ≥ 3:1 for the headline, official logo; reel: narration fits shots, captions ≤ 2 lines × 42 chars, music licence, watched muted on a phone.

**Brand consistency:** one style sentence ("flat vector, navy/orange/gold, rounded shapes") reused in every visual prompt; one key message ("Order ahead, skip the queue") repeated in all pieces; the same fonts and logo file; a shared prompt library.

</details>


### 🧩 Scenario 2 · Planning a hackathon with a deadline

The department's AI hackathon is on Saturday 12 December. Tasks (days, prerequisites): Goals 3 (none); Venue 5 (Goals); Speakers 4 (Goals); Materials 6 (Venue, Speakers); Promotion 7 (Venue); Travel 2 (Speakers); Rehearsal 2 (Materials, Promotion, Travel). An AI assistant claims the project takes 29 days and that Speakers is critical. Check the claim, find the real critical path, and describe how you would use AI and code together for the rest of the plan.

<details><summary>✅ Model solution</summary>

**Forward pass:** Goals 0–3; Venue 3–8; Speakers 3–7; Materials starts at max(8, 7) = 8, ends 14; Promotion 8–15; Travel 7–9; Rehearsal starts at max(14, 15, 9) = 15, ends **17**.

**Backward pass / slack:** Rehearsal LS 15; Promotion LS 8 (slack 0); Materials LS 9 (slack 1); Travel LS 13 (slack 6); Venue LS 3 (slack 0); Speakers LS 5 (slack 2); Goals LS 0.

**Result:** the project takes **17 days**, not 29 (the AI simply added all durations). The critical path is **Goals → Venue → Promotion → Rehearsal**. Speakers has 2 days of slack, so it is **not** critical.

**AI + code workflow:** AI drafts the WBS, invitation emails, promotion posts, run sheet and risk list; code (or a spreadsheet) computes the critical path and back-schedules dated deadlines from 12 December (moving Sunday tasks to Saturday); the team verifies durations and owners (RACI) and adds a 10–20% buffer to critical tasks. Re-run the schedule weekly when anything slips.

</details>


### 🧩 Scenario 3 · Should we switch to reels?

After a month the social media lead shares: Reels — 21 posts, total reach 72,000, engagements 3,280. Text posts — 15 posts, total reach 16,600, engagements 302. An A/B test of two reel hooks: Hook A 36 clicks / 1,200 views, Hook B 54 clicks / 1,200 views. The lead wants to stop text posts and use Hook B on every reel. Analyse the numbers, test the hook result, and give a recommendation with an AI-assisted reporting step.

<details><summary>✅ Model solution</summary>

**Engagement rates (from totals):** Reels 3,280 ÷ 72,000 = **4.56%**; Text 302 ÷ 16,600 = **1.82%**. Reels engage about 2.5× better, with a reasonable sample (21 and 15 posts).

**A/B test:** CTR A = 36/1,200 = 3.0%, CTR B = 54/1,200 = 4.5% (+50% lift). Pooled p = 90/2,400 = 0.0375; SE = √(0.0375 × 0.9625 × (2/1,200)) ≈ 0.00776; z = 0.015 ÷ 0.00776 ≈ **1.93**, p ≈ 0.053. Just **below** the 1.96 threshold, so not yet significant at 95%.

**Recommendation:**

1. Shift most effort to reels (for example 60% of posts), but keep some text/carousel posts for announcements and accessibility; check whether text posts drive clicks or sign-ups before cutting them completely.
2. Keep testing Hook B against A for another ~1,000 views per version before adopting it everywhere; B is promising, not proven.
3. Track sign-ups per post, not only engagement, since applications are the real goal.

**Reporting step:** give an AI assistant the computed table and test result with "use ONLY these numbers; flag low-confidence results", ask for a 150-word summary for the department head, then check every figure in its answer against the table before sending.

</details>
